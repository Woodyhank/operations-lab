01 — THE OBSERVATION

IT operations and industrial operations seem different.

One runs applications, servers and digital services.

The other runs machines, production lines and physical processes.

Yet they face remarkably similar problems.

  • A system fails.
  • A signal is missed.
  • A problem repeats.
  • A team reacts.
  • Production suffers.
THE TECHNOLOGY IS DIFFERENT.
THE OPERATIONAL CHALLENGES ARE NOT.
02 — THE SHIFT

For a long time, IT Operations has been largely focused on what happens after something goes wrong.

  • Incident management
  • Escalation
  • Root cause analysis
  • Problem management
  • Recovery

All necessary. But not enough.

“ Don't just make the system recover.
Design the system so that failure becomes less likely — and less painful.

Industrial environments have spent decades developing this mindset. That changes everything.

03 — WHAT INDUSTRY DOES DIFFERENTLY
01

DESIGN FOR
RELIABILITY

Reliability is not an operations activity. It is a design principle.

Designing for reliability upstream reduces incidents downstream.

02

STANDARD
WORK

Standards are not about limiting people. They are about enabling consistency, quality and repeatability.

03

VISUAL
MANAGEMENT

Problems should become visible quickly. The system should tell you when something is wrong.

Not more data. Better signals.

04

PROBLEM
SOLVING

The better question isn't only “What happened?” but “What allowed this problem to happen?”

05

CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

A reliable operation isn't an operation without incidents. That's impossible.

A reliable operation is one that learns from them.

The incident becomes
an input to the system.

?
